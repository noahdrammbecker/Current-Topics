History

Every establishment has a beginning. Ours began with an ending.

Black Tuesday

On October 29, 1929, the world changed. The great machine of American prosperity ground to a halt, spitting out fortunes like broken teeth. In New York, men in fine suits wept on Wall Street. In Dael City, one banker watched his entire life dissolve into ticker tape and telegrams.

Mr. Frederic Francesco La'Faux had built his career on certainty—on the unwavering belief that numbers never lied and markets never truly fell. That morning, he learned that certainty was simply another luxury the crash had taken.

By noon, he had lost everything. His accounts. His reputation. His standing at the Dael City Commerce Bank. Everything except a small brass key in his pocket that no one knew he possessed, and a Fabergé egg he had no business owning.

"I watched my fortune burn. And in the ashes, I saw opportunity." — F. F. La'Faux, 1931

The Barbershop

One year later, a modest barbershop opened its doors on the riverfront of The Districts. The sign read simply: Balducci's Barbers. The haircuts were honest. The shaves were Sharp(e). The proprietor—one Freddie La'Faux, operating quite openly under his family name—employed a quiet fellow named Teddy to work the chair. Teddy asked no questions, remembered no faces, and handled odd jobs that required neither.

But those who knew—and there were always those who knew—understood that the real business lay beyond the back wall. Behind a mirror framed in tarnished silver. Through a brass-trimmed door that only opened if you knew what to ask for.

That door led to The Gilded Goose.

The Goose

Step through, and the world changes. Gas lamps flicker against emerald wallpaper. Velvet booths line walls paneled in dark mahogany. A stage sits empty most nights, waiting for the jazz trio that only plays when Freddie nods. And there, behind the bar, a glass case housing a single Fabergé egg—golden, impossibly intricate, worth more than most men's lives.

No one knows how Freddie financed the place. Some whisper of European connections. Others speak of debts collected in unconventional currencies. A few—the truly foolish—suggest the egg itself holds the answer.

All know not to ask.

"Freddie works the room like a man who has already seen how the evening ends." — A Regular

The Bartenders

Behind the bar, two figures move in perfect synchronicity. They are known only as Mason and Wilson. No first names. No histories. They appeared the night The Gilded Goose opened and have not, to anyone's knowledge, ever left.

They speak rarely and only to each other—quick whispers in a language no patron has identified. Their hands are pale, their movements precise. They remember every drink you've ever ordered, though you've never seen them write anything down.

Some say they're brothers. Some say they're something older. The woman in booth seven claims she saw Mason pour a drink in 1919, in a different city, in a bar that burned down a decade ago. She has not been seen since.

Freddie trusts them implicitly. That alone should tell you something.

"I asked Wilson where he came from once. He smiled, and for a moment, I forgot the question entirely." — Anonymous

Dael City

To understand The Gilded Goose, you must first understand Dael City. Built where the Missouri meets its tributary, the city grew fat on stockyards and steel. They call it the Chicago of the heartland—all that ambition, all that industry, transplanted to the prairie.

But Dael City is not Chicago. Chicago has the lake. Dael City has only the river, brown and sluggish, carrying secrets south toward New Orleans. Chicago has its towers. Dael City builds low and wide, its neighborhoods spreading like spilled ink across the floodplain.

And Chicago has its rules. Dael City... Dael City has arrangements.

In 1932, prohibition still holds the nation in its dry grip. But The Gilded Goose pours freely. How? The arrangements. The understanding between Freddie and certain officials. The envelope that passes from gloved hand to uniform pocket every third Thursday.

It's not legal. But in Dael City, legal is simply another word for affordable.

This is The Gilded Goose. You've found your way in.
The only question now is whether you'll find your way out.
